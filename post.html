The Life Cycle of a Star

Category: Stars · Tags: Supernova, Nebula, Main Sequence

Stars are not eternal; they are born, evolve, and eventually die. Their entire life cycle is driven by the balance between gravity pulling inward and pressure from nuclear fusion pushing outward.

1. Stellar Nursery

The story of a star begins in a cold, dense region of a molecular cloud, often called a stellar nursery. Gravity slowly pulls gas and dust together into clumps that grow more massive and hotter.

2. Protostar

As the clump contracts, it forms a protostar. It is not yet performing nuclear fusion in its core, but it shines faintly due to the heat generated by gravitational collapse.

3. Main Sequence Star

When the core temperature becomes high enough, hydrogen nuclei begin to fuse into helium. The star joins the main sequence, where it will spend most of its life in a stable balance of forces.

4. Red Giant or Supergiant

Once the hydrogen in the core is depleted, fusion slows, and the core contracts. The outer layers expand and cool, and the star becomes a red giant (for Sun-like stars) or a red supergiant (for very massive stars).

5. The End: White Dwarf, Neutron Star, or Black Hole

The final stage depends on the mass of the star. Low-mass stars gently shed their outer layers and leave behind a white dwarf. More massive stars may explode as supernovae, leaving neutron stars or black holes.

Quick Facts

  • Our Sun is a G-type main-sequence star.
  • Stars spend about 90% of their life on the main sequence.
  • Mass determines a star’s entire evolution.
